Privacy Policy

What we collect, why we collect it, and how to make us delete it.

Last updated: 27 July 2026 · Operated by Kyle Maloney, Illinois, United States · Contact: wiki.guesser.online@gmail.com

The short version

Wiki-Guesser is a Wikipedia guessing game. We collect as little as we can get away with:

  • We never ask for your email address — not even when you sign in with Google or Discord.
  • We never see your password. Signing in happens on Google's or Discord's site, not ours.
  • Chat messages are never written to disk — unless someone reports one. Chat passes through memory to the other players in your room and is gone. The single exception is a message a player reports to us for moderation, which we keep for a short time so we can act on it.
  • No advertising, no analytics, no tracking cookies. The only cookie we set is the one that keeps you signed in.
  • You can play entirely as a guest, in which case we store no account for you at all.
  • You can delete your account yourself, instantly, from your profile — no email, no waiting.

The rest of this page is the detail. If anything here is unclear, ask us at wiki.guesser.online@gmail.com and we'll explain it.

What we collect

If you play as a guest

You pick a display name and we generate a random guest ID. Both live in your session record (see Cookies) and are shown to the other players in your room. We create no account and no permanent record of you. When the session expires, it's gone.

If you sign in with Google or Discord

We ask those providers for the minimum profile scope and store only what the game needs to show you on a scoreboard:

We do not request or store your email address, contact list, friends, servers, posts, or anything else. We never receive your password. You can revoke our access at any time in your Google account permissions or Discord authorised apps.

Gameplay and ranking data

If you play ranked matches with an account, we store your rating, games played, and win/loss/draw counts for each ladder, plus one row per completed ranked match recording the mode, both players, the scores, the resulting ratings, and when it finished. This is what makes a ladder a ladder — without it there is no ranking. Casual and private-room games are not recorded.

Daily puzzle scores

If you finish a daily puzzle, we store one row for that day: your display name at the time, how many words it took, how long it took, and which day it was. Guests are included — the daily leaderboard is open to everyone, so it is not tied to having an account.

The row records the name as it was when you played, so the board keeps reading correctly afterwards. It is not a history: daily scores are deleted automatically after 30 days, because a scoreboard for one day stops meaning anything once the day has passed. If you have an account and delete it, your daily scores go with it immediately, along with everything else.

Chat messages

Chat is relayed in memory to the other players in your room and is not written to a database or log file. We keep no general history of it and cannot go back and read a conversation.

Reported messages are the one exception. When a player uses the report button on a message, that single message is written to our server log so a human can look at it. We record the message text, the display name and account ID of whoever sent it, the display name and account ID of whoever reported it, the room, and the time. Nothing else from the conversation is kept — not the messages around it, and not the messages nobody reported.

We do this to keep the game usable: without it, a report would tell us that something happened but never what. Our lawful basis is our legitimate interest in protecting players from abuse. Reported messages are deleted with the rest of our server logs, on the schedule in the table below.

You can turn chat off for yourself at any time from your profile or from the chat panel in a game, and the host of a private room can switch it off for everyone in that room.

Server logs and security data

Our server writes a log line for each web request containing your IP address, the time, the page requested, the response status, and how long it took. We also count requests per IP address in memory to stop one person flooding the service. IP addresses are personal data, and we treat them as such: we use them only to keep the service up and to investigate abuse, and we delete the logs on the schedule below.

Cookies and local storage

We use one cookie and one item of browser local storage. Neither tracks you, and neither is shared with anyone. Because both are strictly necessary to provide a service you asked for, they do not require a consent banner under the ePrivacy Directive — but you should still know they exist:

We run no advertising, no analytics, and no third-party tracking of any kind. If that ever changes we will update this policy and, where the law requires it, ask for your consent before setting any non-essential cookie.

Who your data reaches

We do not sell your data, and we do not share it for anyone else's marketing. It reaches these parties only:

  • Google or Discord — only if you choose to sign in with them. They necessarily learn that you signed in to Wiki-Guesser. Their handling is governed by Google's privacy policy and Discord's privacy policy.
  • Wikimedia Foundation — article images are loaded by your browser directly from Wikimedia's servers rather than proxied through us. That means Wikimedia receives your IP address and browser user-agent when an image loads, exactly as if you had visited Wikipedia. See the Wikimedia privacy policy.
  • Amazon Web Services — our hosting provider, based in the United States. They store the data on our behalf under their standard data processing terms.
  • Other players — your display name, avatar, score, and rating are visible to people in your game, and appear on the public leaderboard if you play ranked.
  • If the law requires it — we will disclose data where we are legally obliged to, and will tell you unless we are forbidden from doing so.

Your rights

If you are in the UK or EEA you have the right to access your data, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict or object to how we use it, and to receive it in a portable format. Where we rely on legitimate interests, you can object and we will stop unless we have compelling grounds not to.

We extend these same rights to everyone, wherever you live — it would be strange to do otherwise for a game. If you are a California resident, you also have rights under the CCPA/CPRA, including to know what we collect and to request deletion. We do not sell or share personal information as those terms are defined there.

You can delete your account yourself, immediately. Sign in, click your name in the top-right corner to open your profile, and use Delete my account at the bottom. It takes effect the moment you confirm — there is no queue, no grace period, and no soft-delete we could reverse. You do not have to ask us, and you do not have to give a reason.

That erases, in a single database transaction: your profile row (provider, provider ID, display name, avatar URL, signup date), every ladder rating and win/loss record you hold, every ranked match you played, and every daily puzzle score standing in your name — which also takes you off any daily leaderboard you were on. Because a match record names both players, deleting yours also removes those games from your opponents' histories — we would rather shorten someone's match list than keep your game record against your wishes. Their own rating and win/loss totals are stored separately and are not affected.

For anything else — access, correction, restriction, objection, or a portable copy — email wiki.guesser.online@gmail.com from an account you can demonstrate control of, or tell us your in-game display name and which provider you signed in with. We'll respond within 30 days.

We also delete accounts that have gone 24 months without being used, along with their ratings and match history. This runs as an automatic daily job, not as something we remember to do. Simply signing in counts as using the account and resets the clock.

If you think we've mishandled your data you can complain to your local supervisory authority — in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office. We'd appreciate the chance to fix it first.

International transfers

Our servers are in the United States. If you access the game from the UK or EEA, your data is transferred there. We rely on the UK/EU Standard Contractual Clauses with our hosting provider as the safeguard for that transfer.

Children

Wiki-Guesser is not intended for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their data. In some EU countries the age of digital consent is 16 — if you are under the age of consent where you live, please use the game only with a parent or guardian's permission. If you believe a child has given us data, email us and we'll delete it.

Security

We serve the site over HTTPS, mark session cookies HttpOnly and SameSite, apply a strict Content Security Policy, and rate-limit requests. We never handle your password. No system is perfectly secure, and we won't pretend otherwise — but we keep so little data that there is very little to lose.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy we'll update the date at the top. If the change is significant — new categories of data, a new third party, or anything involving advertising — we'll say so prominently on the site before it takes effect.
